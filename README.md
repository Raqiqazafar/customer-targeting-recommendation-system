# Customer Segmentation & Product Recommendation System

A data-driven customer analytics and product recommendation system built using Python, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, SciPy, and association-rule techniques.

The project analyzes customer purchasing behavior, assigns customer segments based on RFM and a customer value score, identifies actionable customer groups, and generates personalized product recommendations using historical co-purchase behavior.

---

## Project Overview

Understanding customer behavior is important for targeted marketing, customer retention, and personalized product recommendations.

This project combines customer-level analytics with a recommendation approach to answer questions such as:

- Which customers are the most valuable?
- Which customers are becoming inactive?
- Which customers have high growth potential?
- Which customers can be targeted for cross-selling?
- Which products are commonly purchased together?
- How well does the recommendation system perform on unseen transactions?

The project follows a complete analytics workflow from data cleaning and customer analysis to segmentation, recommendation generation, and model evaluation.

## Dataset

The project uses a retail transaction dataset with transaction-level customer purchase information (541,909 records, December 2010 to December 2011).

### Main Features

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| InvoiceNo | Transaction/invoice identifier |
| StockCode | Product identifier |
| Description | Product description |
| Quantity | Number of units purchased |
| InvoiceDate | Transaction date and time |
| UnitPrice | Price per unit |
| CustomerID | Customer identifier |
| Country | Customer's country |

> The raw dataset is not included in this repository.

### Data Cleaning

The following records are removed before analysis:

- Rows with missing `CustomerID`
- Rows with non-positive `Quantity` or `UnitPrice`
- Duplicate rows
- Cancelled invoices (InvoiceNo starting with `C`)
- Non-product stock codes (`POST`, `DOT`, `M`, `BANK CHARGES`) in the revenue and RFM analysis

After cleaning, the data is reduced from 541,909 rows to 392,692 rows covering 4,338 customers.

## RFM Analysis

Customer purchasing behavior is analyzed using three key dimensions:

- **Recency:** Days since the customer's last purchase
- **Frequency:** Number of unique transactions (invoices)
- **Monetary:** Total revenue generated by the customer

RFM scores (1 to 5) are calculated using quintile-based scoring. Recency is scored in reverse order so that more recent customers receive higher scores, while higher frequency and monetary values receive higher scores.

---

## Customer Value Scoring

A Customer Value Score combines the three major dimensions of customer behavior.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Recency | 40% |
| Frequency | 30% |
| Monetary | 30% |

Frequency and Monetary values are log-transformed (`log1p`) before Min-Max normalization to reduce the effect of highly skewed values. Recency is normalized and inverted so that recent customers score higher.

The resulting score is used to rank customers by overall value.

## Customer Segmentation

Customers are assigned to segments using rules applied **in the following order** (the first matching rule wins):

| Order | Segment | Rule |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | VIP / Loyal | R ≥ 4, F ≥ 4, M ≥ 4 |
| 2 | High Value - Win Back | R ≤ 2 and M ≥ 4 |
| 3 | High Potential | Customer Value Score in the top 25% (≥ 75th percentile) |
| 4 | Active - Cross Sell | R ≥ 4 and F ≥ 3 |
| 5 | Nurture | All remaining customers |

### Segment Actions and Priority

| Segment | Business Action | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| VIP / Loyal | Retain & Reward | High |
| High Potential | Upsell & Loyalty Program | High |
| High Value - Win Back | Win-Back Campaign | High |
| Active - Cross Sell | Cross-Sell Products | Medium |
| Nurture | Re-Engagement / Nurture | Low |

## Business Insights & Customer Actions

### VIP / Loyal
Customers showing strong recent activity, purchase frequency, and monetary value.
**Action:** Retain & Reward

### High Potential
Customers with strong overall customer value who represent an opportunity for further growth.
**Action:** Upsell & Loyalty Program

### Active - Cross Sell
Recently active customers who can be targeted with relevant additional products.
**Action:** Cross-Sell Products

### High Value - Win Back
Previously valuable customers whose recent purchasing activity has declined.
**Action:** Win-Back Campaign

### Nurture
Customers with comparatively lower engagement or value.
**Action:** Re-Engagement / Nurture

## Product Recommendation System

The recommendation component is based on historical customer-product purchasing behavior. The association rules are implemented directly with sparse matrices (SciPy) rather than an external association-rule library.

The workflow includes:

1. Customer-product interaction analysis
2. Product popularity analysis (products bought by at least 50 customers)
3. Co-purchase analysis using a sparse customer-product matrix
4. Association-based recommendation generation
5. Train/test evaluation
6. Popularity-based fallback recommendations

### Rule Selection

Product pairs are kept as "strong" rules when they satisfy all of the following:

- Bought together by at least **100** customers
- **Confidence ≥ 0.50** in at least one direction
- **Lift ≥ 3**

Each rule is then scored:

```text
Recommendation Score = 0.5 × Confidence
                     + 0.3 × (Lift / max Lift)
                     + 0.2 × (Co-purchase Count / max Co-purchase Count)
```

For a customer, rules triggered by their purchased products are ranked by score, products already purchased are excluded, and the top 5 are returned.

---

## Recommendation Evaluation

The recommendation system is evaluated using a time-based train/test approach. The first 80% of transactions (by date) are used to build the model, and the later 20% are used to evaluate recommendations on unseen purchases. 1,883 customers are evaluated.

### Hit Rate@5
Percentage of evaluated customers for whom at least one recommended product appears in their test purchases.

### Recall@5
Average share of a customer's test purchases that are captured by the top-5 recommendations.

### Coverage@5
Percentage of evaluated customers for whom the system generates at least one recommendation.

---

## Recommendation Models

### Baseline Association Rules
Generates product recommendations using association relationships learned from training transactions.

### Association + Popularity Fallback
Uses association-based recommendations when available and fills the remaining slots with popular products the customer has not bought, so every customer receives recommendations.

---

## Model Comparison

| Model | Hit Rate@5 | Recall@5 | Coverage@5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline Association Rules | 19.44% | 0.98% | 78.17% |
| Association + Popularity Fallback | 25.07% | 1.33% | 100.00% |

**Note on Recall@5:** Recall is naturally low because many customers buy a large number of different products in the test period (some over 100), while only 5 products are recommended. Hit Rate is the more intuitive indicator here: about 1 in 4 customers receives at least one product they actually went on to buy.
